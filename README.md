# Looking for Great Work

English | [中文](README_zh.md)

The number of papers in our field is exploding. Most of them will not be read a year from now, and adding one more does not change much.

Prof. Cao's team is looking for great work, not for more papers. We use Paul Graham's essay as the standard:

**How to Do Great Work** — https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html

The essay is not only about research. It applies equally to building products and start-ups. His working definition: great work means doing something important so well that you expand people's ideas of what is possible. We hope it will help you find important and meaningful projects for your time and career.

## 1. Primary questions

Write the following as a short note of its own. It is submitted separately from your proposal, so the proposal itself can stay as it is: a proposal, a report or a paper draft are all fine.

### Your project, in one paragraph

Explain what you want to do in plain words. Leave out acronyms and fashionable terms where you can. A reader from outside your subfield should understand what you claim and why it matters.

### Three questions

The three questions follow Paul Graham's own list:

> "The work you choose needs to have three qualities: it has to be something you have a natural aptitude for, that you have a deep interest in, and that offers scope to do great work."

He also says these multiply: "The factors in doing great work are factors in the literal, mathematical sense, and they are: ability, interest, effort, and luck." If one factor is zero, so is the product.

Answer each one in 1–2 sentences. You may summarise, point to a section of your document, or quote from it. "I don't know yet" is an acceptable answer and is more useful than a polished sentence that says nothing.

**1. Scope and ambition**

If this works completely, what will be different? There are two ways to pass: a real application that changes how people do something, or a real change in what the research community understands. A few points on a benchmark is neither. Think about the whole world, including existing work and other teams on the same problem: will your result be one more paper, or something others cannot go around?

Before you answer, ask whether Claude Code or Codex could do this in a day if you simply asked. If so, that is your baseline, not your project.

The first version can be small. The question is about where the project points, not how far it has gone.

**2. Curiosity**

What do you most want to know about this problem? We are asking for the thing you want to find out, not the contribution list of a paper. Would you continue if the paper were rejected? Did the question come from you, or from the future-work section of someone else's paper?

**3. Why you**

Why do you have a real chance of success? Certainty is not required, but there must be a reason to make the bet. What do you have that others do not: data, a tool, knowledge from another field, an observation nobody else has made, a first result that already works? What resources does the project need, and what do you have now?

## 2. Get more feedback on the proposal

When your document is ready, give it to the [`great-work-evaluation`](skills/great-work-evaluation/SKILL.md) skill to have it scored and to get specific feedback. If you also give it your answers to the three questions, it will check them against what it finds.

To install the skill in Claude Code, copy the folder `skills/great-work-evaluation` into `~/.claude/skills/`.
